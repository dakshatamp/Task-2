## B. Suspected Adverse Reaction

### Event / Reaction Start Date

The exact date was not reported. The reaction began approximately 35 days after the initiation of allopurinol (100 mg twice daily).

### Event / Reaction Stop Date

The exact date was not reported. The reaction resolved completely following withdrawal of allopurinol and treatment.

### Description of the Event and Its Management

A 58-year-old hypertensive woman developed fever and chills, followed by progressively worsening generalized erythematous skin lesions accompanied by severe pruritus, a burning sensation and a bitter taste. The onset was approximately 35 days after she started allopurinol for a provisional diagnosis of gout. Examination revealed diffuse erythematous to hyperpigmented scaly plaques over the face, trunk, groin and extremities, with bilateral pitting edema.

Laboratory investigations showed eosinophilia, leukocytosis, transient thrombocytopenia, acute kidney injury and hepatic involvement. The reported RegiSCAR score was 7, consistent with a definite diagnosis of DRESS syndrome.

Allopurinol was discontinued. The patient was treated with intravenous normal saline, systemic corticosteroids, antihistamines and supportive care, followed by oral prednisolone with gradual tapering. Renal and hepatic parameters improved and the patient recovered completely. No rechallenge was performed.

### Relevant Medical and Medication History

The patient had hypertension for 8 years, treated with telmisartan and hydrochlorothiazide. Allopurinol was prescribed at 100 mg twice daily for provisional gout. No other relevant allergy or medical history was reported in the case description. Pregnancy, addiction, and pre-existing hepatic or renal dysfunction were not reported.

### Seriousness of the Reaction

The reaction was serious. It was classified under hospitalization (initial or prolonged) and other medically important reaction. It was not fatal, life threatening, or associated with congenital anomaly or disability. The patient was hospitalized for multi-system involvement, including acute kidney injury, hepatic involvement and cytopenias, and required systemic corticosteroid therapy.

### Outcome

Recovered. The patient made a complete clinical recovery without recurrence.

### Action Taken After the Reaction

Allopurinol was withdrawn. No dose reduction was attempted. Permanent avoidance of allopurinol was advised and a drug alert card was issued to the patient. After stabilization, febuxostat 40 mg/day was started as an alternative urate-lowering treatment.

### Reaction Reappearance After Reintroduction

Effect unknown. Allopurinol was not reintroduced and no rechallenge was performed; therefore the outcome of re-exposure is unknown and should not be interpreted as a negative rechallenge. No dose is applicable.

## Additional Information

### Type of Reaction and Diagnosis

This was a Type B (bizarre / hypersensitivity) adverse drug reaction, presenting as a delayed immune-mediated hypersensitivity reaction in the form of DRESS syndrome. The RegiSCAR score of 7 indicates definite DRESS.

### Causality Assessment

**WHO-UMC assessment:** Probable / Likely. There was a reasonable temporal relationship with allopurinol exposure. The clinical presentation, with eosinophilia and systemic organ involvement, was compatible with the reaction, and no alternative cause was supported by the reported investigations, as infective and autoimmune screens were negative. The reaction improved following withdrawal of the drug and treatment. Rechallenge was not performed.

**Naranjo assessment:** Probable, with a total score of 7 (range 5 to 8). The item-wise scoring is shown below. It has been derived from the case details so that the total corresponds to the reported score.

### Causality Interpretation

The RegiSCAR score of 7 supports a definite diagnosis of DRESS on the basis of the clinical and laboratory features. WHO-UMC and Naranjo, however, address a different question: how likely it is that the suspected medicine caused the reported reaction. The temporal relationship, the characteristic DRESS presentation, the absence of a supported alternative cause and the improvement after withdrawal of allopurinol together support a causal association. Because rechallenge was not performed, the highest level of certainty required for a definite drug-event relationship is not reached. The assessments are therefore recorded as Probable / Likely (WHO-UMC) and Probable (Naranjo).

### Preventive Action

Permanent avoidance of allopurinol was advised and a drug alert card was issued to the patient.
